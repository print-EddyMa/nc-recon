How one building gets its call.

A manufactured home on a Swannanoa lot, record __NCR_SHORT__ at US National Grid __USNG__. Six layers of evidence, stacked.

After 2025

Evidence
Call

  1. Before: a home on its lot.

    NC OneMap statewide orthoimagery, February 2023, 6 inch. The outline comes from FEMA's USA Structures and is aligned to the roof before anything is measured.

  2. After: bare ground.

    NC OneMap post-Helene orthoimagery, spring 2025. The same outline over the same ground, months later.

  3. Change, against the town.

    Groma scores how much the pixels inside the outline changed, then compares that to every other building in Swannanoa. This one is standard deviations above the town's typical change.

  4. The ground, from lidar.

    North Carolina re-flew lidar in 2025. Around this home the ground moved no more than m against the 2017 surface, so the lidar shows no landslide or scour here. Elsewhere in town it flagged buildings.

  5. Evidence, each with its weight.

    The image model calls it Destroyed. Pixel change leans severe. No blue tarp. Flood depth stays empty, because no published river crest sits on this reach. Groma never fills a missing channel with a guess.

  6. The call, and who checks it.

    Destroyed, __P_DESTROYED__%. Severe calls go to people for blind review before they count. Buncombe County's search team, at the site days after the storm, recorded it Destroyed.

What ships today.

Free and open source. Runs on one laptop or a county server, with no cloud account.

Any FEMA disaster

Type a disaster number. Groma reads FEMA's declaration, finds pre-event imagery for any US state and picks the evidence that fits the hazard: flood, wind, wildfire, earthquake or landslide.

Built

Building-level calls

Every structure gets a FEMA level from No damage to Destroyed, a probability for each, the evidence behind it and a review flag when it is unsure.

Built

3D view

Buildings at their inventory height over the imagery and terrain. Heights Groma does not know are drawn as unknown.

Built

Verify

Three people judge each hard call without seeing the model's answer, with Civil Air Patrol photos shot toward the building days after the storm.

Built

Response plan

Roads likely cut, who is cut off, lifelines and water systems at risk, landing-zone candidates and 1 km search sectors, exported as KML for TAK and Google Earth.

Built

FEMA survey export

Calls written into FEMA's damage survey fields, plus GeoPackage, File Geodatabase, KML and CSV with US National Grid.

Built

Offline packs

Carry an assessed area to a field laptop and review it with no network: imagery, basemap, calls and exports. Addresses stay behind.

Beta

Wildfire damage model

Groma's own model, trained on public-domain imagery and CAL FIRE inspections. Used in place of the general image model on wildfires.

A damage count the day imagery arrives.

  1. Start Groma on a laptop or county server, with the commands here.
  2. New assessment: enter the FEMA disaster number, then pick the town on the map.
  3. Send teams by the ranked sectors and review queue. Export to FEMA's survey fields, GeoPackage or KML.
Start it locally
git clone __REPO__ groma
cd groma/pipeline
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements-dev.txt
NCR_DEV_OPEN=1 .venv/bin/python server.py   # API on :8000
cd ../web && npm install
echo "VITE_API_URL=http://127.0.0.1:8000" > .env.development.local
npm run dev                                 # app on :5173

The general image model's weights file is not in the repository; until it is added, that one channel stays off and Groma says so. For a county server, the repository's deployment guide runs it in one container with HTTPS.

Check a building before you pay on it.

  1. Open the building by its US National Grid reference or on the map.
  2. Read the evidence: the probability for each FEMA level and which channel said what.
  3. Export one row per building as CSV or GeoPackage for your claims system.

Checked against people on the ground.

        
15of19

        
When Groma called a Swannanoa home Major or Destroyed, Buncombe County's Helene search teams agreed 15 times out of 19. It found 15 of the 64 homes the teams called Major or Destroyed: flood damage inside a house does not show from above, which is why people check.

        
A comparison on an area used for tuning, not an accuracy figure.

      

      

        
Eaton fire, held out.

        
95.7%

        
Of the Altadena buildings Groma's wildfire model called Major or Destroyed, CAL FIRE's inspectors agreed on 95.7%, and it found 94.2% of the ones they called Major or Destroyed: 2,246 buildings, one run, with the rule fixed before it ran.

        
The model alone against CAL FIRE inspections, not an accuracy claim for the whole system. Eaton burned in the same windstorm as the Palisades fire the model learned from.
