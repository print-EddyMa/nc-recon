## Description

```
After Hurricane Helene, crews logged 80,000 damage assessments by hand, house by house. NC Recon reads public imagery after a disaster and maps every building: which are likely damaged, which roads are likely cut, landing-zone candidates, and which areas to search first.

In Swannanoa, North Carolina, it read 893 buildings in 88 seconds on one laptop. People still check the hard calls.

Free and open source (Apache-2.0): https://github.com/print-EddyMa/nc-recon

What the numbers mean
• 96%: when NC Recon's wildfire model called a building destroyed or badly damaged after the Eaton fire, CAL FIRE inspectors agreed 95.7% of the time (recall 94.2%, 2,246 buildings, one pre-registered held-out test). Flood accuracy is still being validated.
• 88 seconds measures speed, not accuracy.
• NC Recon was built after Helene and was not used in the response. It is decision support: verify on the ground.
```
