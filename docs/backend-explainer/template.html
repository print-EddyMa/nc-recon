NC Recon · inside the backend

From one point on a map to a call on every building

The backend takes a place and a disaster, pulls the before and after imagery, finds every structure, and gives each one a FEMA damage level with a probability. Then it says which of those calls a person has to check. Every figure below is from one real run: Old Fort, North Carolina, after Hurricane Helene.

01

The request

One container runs the API, the worker and the web app. The API never does the heavy work: it queues a job, and the queue runs the pipeline one step at a time as separate processes.

One job, five steps

The two halves fail differently on purpose. The damage assessment is the product, so nothing after it is allowed to throw it away.

Where the 84 seconds go

Inside "assessing structures", from the run's own manifest. Half the time is the classifier reading 1,693 roofs on a CPU; most of the rest is lining outlines up with roofs and measuring change.

02

The picture

Two images of the same ground, one before the disaster and one after. Which two is the first decision, and the backend refuses a pair that does not straddle the incident.

Dates are asserted, not assumed

The incident date comes from the federal declaration. The pre image must fall before it and the post image on or after it, inside the event's windows, or the run stops.

What this pair costs

Old Fort's two Maxar captures were taken from different angles, in different light and seasons. Each difference is measured, written into the output, and charged against the change evidence.

For the other western NC towns the pair is NC OneMap's statewide orthos before and its post-Helene orthos after: same program, same season, and a co-registration residual under one pixel, so change evidence keeps more weight there.

03

Every structure

Three inventories are merged so nothing is silently dropped. A structure the imagery cannot be read for is still counted.

Outlines miss their roofs by metres

Building outlines were traced on other imagery, years earlier. Left where they are, the classifier scores the neighbour's lawn, which reads as "destroyed". So every outline is moved onto its roof first: one shared offset per image, then a refinement per building.

04

The evidence

No single model decides. Up to four independent readings each say how likely the picture is under each FEMA level, and a reading that cannot see says so and drops out.

The five FEMA levels, in real imagery

Old Fort structures before and after, with the level the backend assigned. These are its calls, not ground truth.

Watch one call being made

Pick a structure. Each channel's bars are its likelihood over the five levels; the fusion multiplies them, each raised to its weight, on top of a prior.

06

From structures to people

Damage levels become an estimate of who is displaced and where need is highest. It ranks where to look; it is not an eligibility determination.

07

Access and failure

Access fails closed, and a source being down may cost detail but never the assessment.

Anyone

  • GET /ops/areas
    counts, ranges, caveats, an extent rounded to about 100 m
  • GET …/public.geojson
    by census tract; any count under 5 withheld, with the people figures behind it
  • GET /ops/health · /ops/report
    data-source board, accuracy report
  • GET /feed/{name}
    live conditions from a fixed list of sources

Responders, with the token

  • GET /ops/areas/{id}
    every structure: level, probabilities, evidence, reasons
  • GET …/chip · …/tiles
    before and after imagery
  • GET …/export.zip · …/brief.html · …/responder
    GIS formats, the one-page brief, addresses
  • POST /ops/labels · /ops/assess
    record ground truth; start, cancel or re-run an assessment

08

Guard rails, and eight towns

The same pipeline has run on eight western North Carolina towns. None of it is validated yet, and the rules below are what keep the backend from quietly overstating what it knows.

  • Evaluation areas are fencedAreas are TUNE or TEST. Calibration refuses any TEST label; accuracy is reported per role and never pooled.
  • No labels, no accuracy numberThe report says "not yet validated" and prints nothing else until analyst labels exist.
  • Severe calls are never left aloneUntil the model is calibrated, every Major or Destroyed call goes to a person.
  • Dates are never inventedWhen a source publishes only a season, the output carries the range, and FEMA's image-date field stays empty.
  • Nothing is measured in Web MercatorAnalysis runs in the imagery's projected CRS. Map tiles are pictures only.
  • Everything carries provenanceEach layer records source, licence, retrieval time and steps; each run records input hashes and library versions.
  • Small counts are withheldPublic outputs suppress any count under 5, with the people figures that rest on it.
  • Tests cannot touch live dataThe suite keeps its registry, job list and audit log in a temporary directory. 143 tests pass offline.
